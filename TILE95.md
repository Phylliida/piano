# tile95 — the generated PCB

One self-contained tile of the hexagonal Hall-effect keyboard. Generated by
`gen_tile.py`. Architecture reverse-engineered from `peppapighs/HE60`
(see `HE60_NOTES.md`).

## Files

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `kicad/tile95.kicad_pcb` | the board — full placement and outline, no pad nets |
| `kicad/tile95.net` | the connectivity — 158 components, 406 net nodes |
| `gen_tile.py` | the generator; edit constants and re-run |

## Key field: fixed, and the constraints that bind

Both defects below were found by the audits and are now **fixed** in the
generated board. What remains is a set of hard constraints worth understanding,
because they fight each other.

### Fixed: the field is now a real hex field

Odd columns are offset half a row from even ones. Verified from the board:
column start offsets are `[12.5, 25.0]` — two values, so the stagger exists.

### Fixed: the caps fit

| constraint | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| cap across flats | 21.0 mm | project spec |
| cap across **points** | **24.25 mm** | a hexagon's min width over all rotations is its across-flats, but its *max* is across-points, and that is what two caps in the same column have to clear |
| row pitch | **25.0 mm** | must exceed 24.25 → 25.0 is the honest minimum |
| column pitch | 25.0 mm | must exceed the 21 mm across-flats + 4.0 mm clearance |

The row pitch is the binding one. I had 19.05 mm (a standard keyboard row pitch),
which overlaps a 21 mm cap by 1.95 mm. My generator never modelled keycaps, so
nothing caught it.

### The constraint that actually decides the tile count

**The AT32F405 has 12 usable ADC pins.** Its ADC channel table maps channels
0-15 to PA0-PA7, PB0, PB1, PC0-PC5 — but PC1/PC2/PC3 are taken by the three mux
select lines, leaving 12. At one mux per ADC channel and 8 channels per mux,
that caps a tile at **96 keys and 12 muxes**.

Combined with the 250 x 250 mm assembly limit and the 24.25 mm cap constraint:

| tile | keys | muxes | board | tiles needed | total |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| **chosen** | **68** | **9** | **225 x 240** | **5** | **340** |
| alternative | 68 | 9 | 225 x 240 | 4 | 272 — **short of 280** |

**Four tiles cannot reach 280 keys.** 68 x 4 = 272. So the instrument is
**5 tiles**, using 280 of 340 available positions. That is 5 connections to the
hub instead of 3, and it is forced by the cap pitch and the ADC pin count, not by
preference.

Nine rows would give 76 keys/tile and 4 tiles, but the field alone is 200 mm and
the component band needs ~30 mm, so the board lands at 255 mm — 5 mm over.

## Why the seam is invisible

The margin is **derived, not chosen**. For the pitch across a seam to equal the
pitch inside a tile:

```
outline -> first column  =  MARGIN
last column -> outline   =  MARGIN
across a seam the halves add:  2 * MARGIN  =  COL_PITCH
therefore  MARGIN = COL_PITCH / 2
```

Getting this wrong is not subtle. Earlier iterations produced a **23.000 mm**
seam against a **25.111 mm** internal pitch — a 2 mm error that would read as a
visible gap and an uneven key. The fix was to stop treating the margin as a
layout preference and anchor it to the pitch, measured from the outline.

Two further conditions the layout satisfies:
- **Even column count (10)** so the seam falls on a plain column boundary.
- **Alternating 9/10 rows** so three tiles assemble into a hexagon.

**The 199 ceiling is a measurement trap.** `kicad-cli pcb drc` stops reporting a
given rule at 199 violations (verified: the JSON output truncates identically
mid-list, at `C41`, then resumes at the power section's `C141`). A rule that sits
at exactly 199 has an unknown true count, and before/after comparison of such a
rule is worthless. Only rules below 199 can be compared. Every rule that carries
an *error* severity on this board is below 199 and is at zero.

## Decoupling

Added as open item 7: 148 x 0402, all `B.Cu`, all placed by `decoupling_parts()`
in `gen_tile.py`, which is the single source both the board and the `.net` are
built from.

| count | value | nets | serves | HE60 source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `C1`-`C140`,`C148`-`C155` 68 of them | 4.7n | 1=HE*n*, 2=GND | one per Hall sensor, on its output | HE60's 69 x 4.7n (C2, C4 ... C138) |
| 77 of them | 100n | 1=+3V3A, 2=GND | 68 sensors + 9 muxes | HE60's 79 x 100n on +3.3VA |
| 3 of them | 100n | 1=+3V3, 2=GND | one per MCU VDD pin (1, 36, 64) | HE60's C154/C155/C156 |

HE60's pattern, read out of `work/HE60.net`, is one 4.7n across each sensor's
output AND one 100n on that sensor's supply, plus one 100n per mux — so 148 caps
is the count the design asks for, not a bug. The 4.7n sits below its sensor and
the 100n above it, both 4.0 mm out on the sensor's centre line.

### The switch's locating pegs, and why the caps are not beside the sensor

The obvious placement — the two caps flanking the sensor at +/-4.0 mm in x — is
wrong, and the DRC is the only thing that says so. The `MX-1U` template carries
two NPTH pads at `(+/-5.08, 0)` with 1.75 mm drills, and a 0402 whose pad edge
lands at 4.0 + 0.76 = 4.76 mm is inside *half a millimetre* of a 5.08 mm hole
that needs 0.25 mm clearance. That placement produced **136 `hole_clearance` and
136 `solder_mask_bridge` violations, one pair per key-field cap**. Nothing else
caught it: `courtyards_overlap` was 0, because `MX-1U` has no courtyard at all,
and the switch is on `F.Cu` while the caps are on `B.Cu`, so only the drill
cares. Along y there is nothing in the switch whatsoever, and the nearest
neighbour is the next key row 25 mm away; the smallest pad-to-hole gap is now
4.9 mm (measured over all 136 holes).

The cap rotation is load-bearing too. A `cap100n`'s Reference field sits 3.8 mm
from the part, so at rot 90 the text of the *lower* cap lands back on its own
sensor's silkscreen: 58 `silk_overlap` warnings, again one per 4.7n. Rotating the
lower caps to 270 and the upper to 90 points both text fields away from the
sensor and drops it to 3.

### Deliberately not copied from HE60

- **HE60's own cap offsets.** Its caps sit 2.6 mm from the sensor, where the two
  courtyards overlap. HE60 ships with no courtyard check; this board keeps
  `courtyards_overlap` at 0, so 4.0 mm it is.
- **`C158` (100n on +3.3VA, next to the MCU) and `C160` (100n on `NRST`)**, which
  HE60 has and this tile does not. The brief scoped MCU decoupling to the three
  VDD pins; these two are the analog-rail and reset caps. Note `+3V3A` reaches
  `U1` pin 13 here, so a `C158` equivalent is the one HE60 part that is arguably
  missing from this pass.
- The mux caps sit at `(mux_x, mux_y + 6.0)`, not at HE60's measured
  `(-3.6, +3.0)` — same reason as the sensors.

## Power section

Added last, on `B.Cu` in the free band between the muxes and `J1`. Every part,
value and net is copied from HE60; the "HE60 ref" column is HE60's own
designator, which is what to look up in `refs/HE60/HE60/HE60.kicad_pcb`.

| tile ref | value | footprint | HE60 ref | nets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `F1` | BSMD0805-050-24V | `Fuse:Fuse_0805_2012Metric` | F1 | 1=VBUS, 2=5V |
| `U5` | USBLC6-2SC6 | `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23-6` | U1 | 1=USB_PLUG_D-, 2=GND, 3=USB_D-, 4=USB_D+, 5=5V, 6=USB_PLUG_D+ |
| `U2` | TLV75733PDBV | `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23-5` | U2 | 1=5V, 2=GND, **3=5V (/EN)**, 5=+3V3A (4 NC) |
| `U3` | XC6206P332MR-G | `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23-3` | U3 | 1=GND, 2=+3V3, 3=5V |
| `C141`-`C144` | 2.2u | `Capacitor_SMD:C_0603_1608Metric` | C148, C150 | 1=5V, 2=GND |
| `C145` | 10u | `Capacitor_SMD:C_0603_1608Metric` | C157 | 1=+3V3, 2=GND |
| `C146` | 2.2u | `Capacitor_SMD:C_0603_1608Metric` | C149 | 1=+3V3A, 2=GND |
| `C147` | 1u | `Capacitor_SMD:C_0603_1608Metric` | C159 | 1=+3V3A, 2=GND |

Placement (all `B.Cu`, rotation 0 delta — the templates' own authored rotation is
kept): `F1` 133.0/195.6, `U5` 143.0/195.5, `U2` 152.0/195.5, `U3` 161.0/195.5,
`C141`-`C144` 133.0/137.5/142.0/146.5 at 203.9, `C145` 154.0/203.9, `C146`
152.0/209.0, `C147` 156.5/209.0.

`+3V3` had to move onto the analog LDO's output: HE60 feeds `+3.3V` from
`XC6206` (`U3`) and `+3.3VA` from `TLV75733` (`U2`), and the tile's MCU VDD pins
are on `+3V3`, so `U3.2` now drives it. Verified driven in `tile95.net`:
`+3V3` = U1.1/36/64 + U3.2 + C145.1, `+3V3A` = 81 nodes incl. U2.5, `5V` =
F1.2 + U2.1 + **U2.3 (/EN)** + U3.3 + U5.5 + four caps.

`U2.3` is `/EN` and needs tying high or the analog rail never comes up; HE60 ties
it to `+5V` and so does this board. The board and `tile95.net` were diffed
pad-by-pad as a set and agree on all 407 pad-nets — they are built by two
independent code paths (the emitter's `nets` dict and `write_netlist()`), so
**any change to one must be mirrored in the other.** Getting `U2.3` into the
board but not the netlist is exactly the mistake that check catches.

Two deliberate deviations from HE60:

- **`VBUS` is new, and J1 pins 1/2 were reassigned to it** (they were `5V`).
  HE60 takes its fuse input from a separate JST `J2` pin 1; this tile has only
  `J1`, so without the reassignment `F1`'s two pads would both have landed on
  `5V` and the fuse would not have been in series at all. Same reasoning put J1
  pins 5/6 on the raw `USB_PLUG_D+`/`USB_PLUG_D-` pair: that is what makes `U5`
  sit *between* the connector and the MCU. Both pairs read correctly in the
  netlist — `USB_PLUG_D+` = J1.5 + U5.6, `USB_D+` = U5.4 + U1.35.
- **The decoupling pass is done** (it was open item 7); see the next section.
  Bulk caps on the three rails were the power pass's job and stay there.

### Two silent failures found and fixed while adding this

Both shipped a board that KiCad loads without complaint:

1. **Every footprint's designator was the template's.** `emit_footprint()`'s
   rename pattern `\(property "Reference" "[^"]*"\)` needs the closing paren on
   the same line, but KiCad writes the group across several lines, so it matched
   nothing: 68 switches were all `MX36`, 68 sensors all `HE38`, nine muxes all
   `AM1`, the MCU was `U4`, and `J1` had no Reference property at all. Fixed in
   `emit_footprint()` — it now finds the property with `sexp_at()` and replaces
   **only the quoted value**, leaving `(at ...)`, `(layer ...)` and `(effects
   ...)` intact. Replacing the whole group instead is the obvious-looking fix and
   is wrong: it strips every field's position and layer. Do not re-tighten that
   regex. The board now has 151 unique designators, `SW1`-`SW68`, `HE1`-`HE68`,
   `AM1`-`AM9`, `U1`-`U5`, `F1`, `J1`.
2. **`make_connector_footprint()` emitted no Reference/Value property at all**,
   which is why `J1` had none. Copied HE60's `J2` property block.

The 240-line detour this cost is the same lesson twice over: verify the output,
not the code path. And when a regex "finds nothing", print the raw text before
touching the pattern — `sexp_at()` + raw print is what settled every one of
these, and three separate parsers of mine were wrong before it was.

### A correction to an earlier version of this document

It previously said the 321 clearance violations were "netclass config" and
concluded "**No real errors**". That was wrong and dangerous. They were
overlapping copper: every mux had its pads shorted (row A shorted five sensor
signals to `MUX_OUT` and GND; row B shorted `+3V3A` to the select lines and four
channels) and all ten J1 pads were shorted to each other. The board could not
have worked.

Root cause: the footprint templates carry the placement rotation they were
authored at (the mux is `(at ... -90)` with pads at 270 degrees) and the
generator re-placed them at 0 degrees — rotating every pad POSITION while
leaving its ORIENTATION behind, so a 1.475 x 0.4 mm land on a 0.65 mm pitch lay
across its neighbour. Fixed by placing each part at its authored rotation. **Do
not "simplify" that back to zero.**

## Connectivity is now written into the board

The board carries real nets: 91 declarations, 406 of 590 pads netted. The 184
without are: 136 switch pads (68 switches, no electrical connection by design --
they are magnets in a housing), 44 MCU pads (the SWD/BOOT0/crystal/OTGHS1_R and
spare-pin gaps of open items 3-5), and 4 J1 pads (spare signals 9/10 and the two
mount pegs). Every sensor, mux, capacitor and power-part pad is netted.

Writing them took three attempts because KiCad 9 is strict about where
`(net N "NAME")` sits inside a pad, and **two of my regexes were silently
wrong**:

1. Placing the attribute right after the pad name parses but yields a corrupt
   netlist. It must come **after `(layers ...)`**.
2. A replacement lambda wrote `(property "Reference" "HE1"` without a closing
   paren. That unbalanced the s-expression, `sexp_at()` then returned `None`,
   and **every net was skipped without a single error being raised.**
3. `\(layers "[^"]*"\)` matches only ONE quoted layer. The real clause is
   `(layers "B.Cu" "B.Mask" "B.Paste")`, so the match failed on every pad.

All three failed silently. The lesson: verify the *output*, not the code path.

## How to use it

1. Open `kicad/tile95.kicad_pcb` in KiCad 9. Connectivity is already in the
   file -- there is nothing to import.
2. `Tools > Update PCB from Schematic` is NOT needed and will not work: no
   schematic exists. The earlier instruction to import `tile95.net` was wrong;
   `kicad-cli` has no netlist-import command.
3. Route. The hex field is on top, everything else on the bottom.
4. Fix the mux netclass clearance rule if DRC complains, then re-check.

## Open items

1. ~~USB pins unverified.~~ **Resolved.** libhmk's `he60-v2` is the AT32F405
   board (`usb.port = "hs"`), and `make.py` selects `BOARD_USB_HS` which drives
   `OTGHS` — the same port as pins 34/35. The CH334 crystal-free question was a
   red herring; the hub is a separate part of the design.
2. ~~The connector footprint is hand-built and unchecked.~~ **Checked against
   the JST drawing** (`https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/pdf/eng/eSH.pdf`), for
   the `SM10B` side-entry 10-circuit part:

   | dimension | JST datasheet | tile95 | |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | pad pitch | 1.0 mm | 1.000 mm | OK |
   | pad width | 0.6 mm | 0.60 mm | OK |
   | pad length | 1.2 mm | 1.55 mm | **deviation** |
   | circuit span (dimension A) | 9.0 mm | 9.0 mm | OK |
   | mount offset from pad row | 1.9 mm | 1.875 mm | OK |
   | mount span | — | 9.6 mm | from HE60's 4-pin |

   Only deviation is pad length: the JST reference is 1.2 mm, I use 1.55 mm
   because that is what HE60's working board uses. A longer land than the
   reference is normal for a rounded-end SMD pad; set the length to 1.2 in
   `make_connector_footprint()` if you want strict conformance.

   Two real defects were found and fixed during this check:
   - The connector was placed on **F.Cu, the switch side**. It belongs on the
     solder side (`B.Cu`), matching HE60. Fixed.
   - Its pads were still on `F.Cu` after that move — copper on one side, mask on
     the other. KiCad flagged 12 `[padstack]` errors. `flip_to_back()` now
     rewrites every layer reference in the footprint. Fixed.

   Still unverified: the mount-peg span. The drawing's dimension table could not
   be parsed reliably for that row, so 9.6 mm is extrapolated from HE60's
   4-pin part — cross-check against the JST mechanical drawing before ordering.
3. **The MCU is unprogrammable as drawn.** U1 pins 46/49/55 (SWDIO/SWCLK/SWO)
   and 60 (BOOT0) are unconnected, and `NRST` (pin 7) is a single-node net.
   HE60 wires all of them. Without SWD pads and BOOT0 access there is no route
   to program the chip — neither SWD nor the factory DFU bootloader.
4. **No crystal, and the reference firmware blocks on one.** Pins 5/6
   (PF0/HEXT_IN, PF1/HEXT_OUT) are unconnected. `refs/libhmk/src/hardware/
   at32f405xx/board.c` enables HEXT then spins on `crm_hext_stable_wait()` — so
   stock libhmk *hangs at clock init* on this board. Either fit the crystal with
   its load caps or patch the firmware to run crystal-free. Boot failure, not a
   missing part.
5. **Pin 33 (`OTGHS1_R`) floating.** HE60 puts a 12 kOhm resistor from pin 33 to
   GND. Unverified whether the AT32F405 requires it; it is a deviation from the
   working reference either way.
6. ~~No power section.~~ **Resolved.** The tile now carries HE60's power chain:
   `J1` -> `F1` (BSMD0805-050-24V polyfuse) -> `5V` -> `U2`/`U3` LDOs ->
   `+3V3A`/`+3V3`, with `U5` (USBLC6-2SC6) in series on the USB pair. See
   "Power section" below. Still absent: the MCU's own support parts (see
   items 3-5).
7. ~~No decoupling capacitors.~~ **Resolved** — 148 x 0402 were added, one 4.7n
   and one 100n per Hall sensor, one 100n per mux, three for the MCU's VDD pins.
   See "Decoupling". Two HE60 parts are still absent by decision, `C158` and
   `C160`; they are named in that section.
8. **The board carries no netlist.** `tile95.net` is an export, and
   `kicad-cli` has no netlist-import command — the earlier instruction to
   "import the netlist directly" was wrong. There is no schematic outside
   `refs/`, so `Tools > Update PCB from Schematic` has nothing to load. A
   schematic has to be created before connectivity can be applied. This also
   means "0 unconnected pads" above is only meaningful once nets exist.
